This interactive lab has six parts and two closing tabs. Part A trains a neuron by gradient descent on 60 patients of ACDC, step by step, and Part B computes a convolution by hand on a slice of cine cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR). Part C compares four settings against overfitting and turns a slice in steps of a quarter circle, and Part D reads a confusion matrix of three classes. Part A. A neuron learns by gradient descent

Each dot is one of the 60 patients of the ACDC training set, placed by the standardised LV end-diastolic volume index (LVEDVI) and the standardised thickest wall; red dots have a cardiomyopathy, DCM or HCM. The neuron starts with $w = (0, 0)$ and $b = 0$. Press One step or Ten steps: The line where $p = 0.5$ moves, the loss on the right falls, and the panel shows the gradients and the update of the last step. The learning rate sets the size of the steps. The ringed dot is patient 134 of the test set, on which the neuron never trains.

With a very large learning rate, the loss of the neuron jumps up and down. Why?

The gradient points the right way, but a step of η times the gradient is too long: The weights jump past the bottom of the loss to a point on the other side that can be higher. A smaller learning rate shortens the steps.

Part B. Convolution by hand

Choose a slice and a kernel, or type nine weights of your own. Click the slice to place the 3 × 3 window, or press Next position to move it one pixel. The table multiplies the nine pixels under the window with the nine weights and adds the products; the sum is one pixel of the feature map on the right. Run the whole map fills all 4096 positions, with padding 1, and Max pooling halves the map. The slices are the ED frames of three patients of the ACDC test set, 2 mm per pixel.

Pixel × Weight = Product

A blur kernel has nine weights of 1/9. What does it compute at each position?

Nine products of a pixel with 1/9 add up to the mean of the nine pixels. A blur smooths the noise and weakens edges; the kernels for edges have weights of both signs that add up to 0.

Part C. Overfitting and augmentation

The network of the course was trained on the 45 training patients for 60 epochs in four settings. Choose a setting and move the slider to stop the training at another epoch: The panel compares the validation loss at that epoch with the lowest one, which early stopping would keep. The second panel turns the ED frame of a middle slice of patient 134 in steps of a quarter circle, the augmentation of the course.

A mirror image would reverse the sides of the heart, as in the rare mirror-image dextrocardia. The course does not use it.

Augmentation shows the network the 135 training slices in four directions. What does it not do?

Turned copies teach the network that the direction of the view does not matter, but they carry the same 45 hearts. A rare finding needs more patients, more slices of each patient or a class of its own.

In the preset where every patient is called normal, what is the macro F1?

Normal: TP = 30, FP = 60, FN = 0, so F1 = 2 × 30/(60 + 60 + 0) = 0.500. DCM and HCM are never predicted, so their F1 is 0. The macro F1 is (0.500 + 0 + 0)/3 = 0.167, while the accuracy is 30/90 = 0.333.

Part E. Training code drill

Each example below is training or evaluation code written by an AI assistant, or a prompt sent to one. Read it with the six questions of the training loop review and choose its hidden problem.

1.

Prompt: I attach the DICOM files of 40 cardiac MR studies from our hospital, with the names of the patients in the headers. Write a PyTorch network that classifies them.
DICOM headers hold names, dates of birth and protocol numbers, and some images carry them in the pixels. The assistant needs the shape, the pixel size, the range of the values and the labels, with made-up images for a test.

2.

mean, sd = X.mean(), X.std()          # all 270 slices
Xn = (X - mean) / sd
Xtr, Xte = Xn[sets == "train"], Xn[sets == "test"]
The mean and the SD come from all slices, the test slices included. They must come from the training slices only.

3.

class Net(nn.Module):
    ...
    def forward(self, x):
        return F.softmax(self.classifier(self.features(x)), dim=1)

loss = F.cross_entropy(model(xb), yb)
F.cross_entropy expects the raw outputs of the last layer and applies the softmax itself. With a softmax in forward, it is applied twice, the gradients shrink and learning slows down or stalls.

4.

# 270 slices of 90 patients, three slices per patient
idx = np.arange(len(y))
train_idx, test_idx = train_test_split(idx, test_size=0.3, stratify=y, random_state=2026)
The split is made by slice, so the slices of one patient can land in both sets, and the network recognises hearts it has already seen. The split must be made by patient, as in acdc.pairs.

5.

# a screening clinic: 5 patients with HCM among 100
acc = (pred == y_clinic).float().mean().item()
print("Ready for the clinic, accuracy:", round(acc, 3))   # 0.95
With 5 percent HCM, calling every patient normal already gives 0.950. The recall and the F1 of HCM show whether the network finds the disease.

6.

model = CmrCNN()
model.load_state_dict(best_state)
with torch.no_grad():
    pred = model(Xte).argmax(dim=1)
print("Accuracy:", (pred == yte).float().mean().item())
A new model is in training mode. Without model.eval(), dropout switches units off at random and batch normalisation uses the statistics of the test slices, so the accuracy changes from run to run.

7.

def augment(x):
    x = torch.flip(x, dims=[3])          # mirror left and right
    return torch.rot90(x, int(torch.randint(0, 4, (1,))), dims=(2, 3))
A mirror image reverses the sides of the heart, as in the rare mirror-image dextrocardia. Turns by a quarter circle keep the anatomy.

8.

model.eval()
probs = []
for xb in Xte.split(32):
    probs.append(F.softmax(model(xb), dim=1))
p_test = torch.cat(probs).detach().numpy()
The evaluation runs outside torch.no_grad(), so autograd records every operation of every batch. The numbers are right, but memory grows with each batch, and a large test set can run out of it.

    A weak and a strong prompt for the same task
    Both prompts ask for the evaluation of the network of the course on the test slices. The code under each prompt is an example of what an AI assistant may return.
    Weak prompt
    evaluate my cnn on the test images and tell me how good it is
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    course_model = CmrCNN()
course_model.load_state_dict(model.state_dict())
X_all = torch.tensor((X - X.mean()) / X.std(), dtype=torch.float32)
probs = F.softmax(course_model(X_all[te]), dim=1)
accuracy = (probs.argmax(dim=1).numpy() == y[te]).mean()
print("Accuracy:", round(float(accuracy), 3))
    Strong prompt, with the prompt card
    Role: You are helping a cardiologist who does not write code. The code will be pasted into a Jupyter notebook and run there.
Context: The cardiologist wants to judge a network for the groups of ACDC as a clinic would, with one decision for each test patient instead of one for each slice.
Data: The notebook holds a table named "data" with the 90 test slices, three for each of the 30 test patients. Each row is a slice: "patient" gives the patient, "true group" the group given by the experts, and "NOR", "DCM" and "HCM" the probabilities of the three groups, from 0 to 1, that the network gives to the slice in evaluation mode.
Task: Average the three probabilities over the three slices of each patient, take the group with the largest mean as the decision for the patient and compare it with the true group.
Constraints: Use only this table. The code must not read files, download anything from the internet or install programs.
Output: Print the share of the 30 test patients whose decision equals their true group with three decimals, and keep it under the name "answer".
Test: There must be 30 patients, and the three mean probabilities of each patient must add up to 1.
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    means = data.groupby("patient")[["NOR", "DCM", "HCM"]].mean()
truth = data.groupby("patient")["true group"].first()
decision = means.idxmax(axis=1)
answer = round(float((decision == truth).mean()), 3)
print("Accuracy:", answer)

# test: 30 patients, and the three mean probabilities of each add up to 1
print(len(means), np.allclose(means.sum(axis=1), 1))
    Which fields of the strong prompt prevent the errors of the weak one?
    The field Data describes a table that the notebook prepares correctly: the probabilities that the network gives to the test slices in evaluation mode, after a normalisation with the training slices. The assistant therefore does not touch the network or the normalisation. The field Task asks for the mean of the three slices of each patient and one decision per patient, and the test checks the number of patients and the probabilities.

    Check yourself
    Seven questions for self-assessment. They are not graded.
    1. A neuron with ReLU has w = (1.0, −0.5) and b = 0.5 and receives x = (2.0, 1.0). What is its output?
    z = 1.0 × 2.0 − 0.5 × 1.0 + 0.5 = 2.0, and ReLU keeps a positive sum: 2.0. The sigmoid would give 0.881.
    2. For a patient with the label 1, a neuron with the sigmoid gives p = 0.8. What is the gradient of the cross-entropy for a weight whose input is 2.0?
    ∂L/∂w = (p − y) x = (0.8 − 1) × 2.0 = −0.4. The update w − η(−0.4) raises the weight, which raises p for this patient.
    3. A 3 × 3 convolution with stride 1 and no padding runs over a map of 32 × 32, followed by max pooling of 2 × 2. What size comes out?
    The convolution gives (32 − 3)/1 + 1 = 30, and the pooling ⌊(30 − 2)/2⌋ + 1 = 15.
    4. How many parameters does a convolution with 8 input channels, 4 kernels of 3 × 3 and biases have?
    Each of the 4 kernels has 8 × 3 × 3 = 72 weights and one bias: 4 × 72 + 4 = 292.
    5. The training loss keeps falling, while the validation loss rises from epoch 10. Which change does not fight overfitting?
    More epochs let the network learn its training slices even more by heart. Augmentation, dropout and early stopping all work against overfitting.
    6. A network flags 20 patients as HCM, 15 of them rightly, and misses 5 patients with HCM. What are its precision, recall and F1 for HCM?
    P = 15/20 = 0.75 and R = 15/(15 + 5) = 0.75. When the two are equal, the F1 equals them: 2 × 15/(30 + 5 + 5) = 0.75.
    7. Why must model.eval() be called before a network with dropout and batch normalisation is measured?
    In training mode, dropout drops random units and batch normalisation uses the statistics of the batch. torch.no_grad() turns the gradients off; model.eval() does not.
