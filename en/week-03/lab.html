Part A. A neuron learns by gradient descent

Each dot is one of 200 patients of the registry, placed by the standardised diameter of the left ventricle and the thickness of the septum; red dots have an abnormal report. The neuron starts with $w = (0, 0)$ and $b = 0$. Press One step or Ten steps: The line where $p = 0.5$ moves, the loss on the right falls, and the panel shows the gradients and the update of the last step. The learning rate sets the size of the steps.

With a very large learning rate, the loss of the neuron jumps up and down. Why?

The gradient points the right way, but a step of η times the gradient is too long: The weights jump past the bottom of the loss to a point on the other side that can be higher. A smaller learning rate shortens the steps.

Part B. Convolution by hand

Choose a frame and a kernel, or type nine weights of your own. Click the frame to place the 3 × 3 window, or press Next position to move it one pixel. The table multiplies the nine pixels under the window with the nine weights and adds the products; the sum is one pixel of the feature map on the right. Run the whole map fills all 4096 positions, with padding 1, and Max pooling halves the map.

Pixel × Weight = Product

A blur kernel has nine weights of 1/9. What does it compute at each position?

Nine products of a pixel with 1/9 add up to the mean of the nine pixels. A blur smooths the speckle and weakens edges; the kernels for edges have weights of both signs that add up to 0.

Part C. Overfitting and augmentation

The network of the course was trained on 300 frames for 40 epochs in four settings. Choose a setting and move the slider to stop the training at another epoch: The panel compares the validation loss at that epoch with the lowest one, which early stopping would keep. The second panel turns, shifts and brightens the frame of VC-1212 within the limits of the course.

A mirror image puts the right ventricle on the wrong side of the heart. No patient looks like this, so the course does not use it.

Augmentation shows the network many copies of the 31 training frames with a thick septum alone. What does it not do?

Rotated and shifted copies teach the network that the position does not matter, but they carry the same 31 hearts. A rare finding needs more patients, a higher resolution or a class of its own.

In the preset where every slice is called normal, what is the macro F1?

Normal: TP = 60, FP = 120, FN = 0, so F1 = 2 × 60/(120 + 120 + 0) = 0.500. DCM and HCM are never predicted, so their F1 is 0. The macro F1 is (0.500 + 0 + 0)/3 = 0.167, while the accuracy is 60/180 = 0.333.

Part E. Training code drill

Each example below is training or evaluation code written by an AI assistant, or a prompt sent to one. Read it with the five questions of the training loop review and choose its hidden problem.

1.

mean, sd = X.mean(), X.std()          # all 2000 frames
Xn = (X - mean) / sd
train_idx, test_idx = train_test_split(np.arange(len(y)), test_size=0.2, stratify=y, random_state=2026)
The mean and the SD come from all frames, the test frames included, before the split. They must come from the training frames only, after the split.

2.

model = EchoCNN()
model.load_state_dict(torch.load("echo_cnn.pt", weights_only=True))
with torch.no_grad():
    pred = model(Xte).argmax(dim=1)
print("F1:", f1_score(yte, pred))
A new model is in training mode. Without model.eval(), dropout switches units off at random and batch normalisation uses the statistics of the test batch, so the F1 changes from run to run.

3.

model.eval()
probs = []
for xb in Xte.split(32):
    probs.append(F.softmax(model(xb), dim=1)[:, 1])
p_test = torch.cat(probs).detach().numpy()
The evaluation runs outside torch.no_grad(), so autograd records every operation of every batch. The numbers are right, but memory grows with each batch, and a large test set can run out of it.

4.

class Net(nn.Module):
    ...
    def forward(self, x):
        return F.softmax(self.classifier(self.features(x)), dim=1)

loss = F.cross_entropy(model(xb), yb)
F.cross_entropy expects the raw outputs of the last layer and applies the softmax itself. With a softmax in forward, it is applied twice, the gradients shrink and learning slows down or stalls.

5.

for epoch in range(30):
    train_one_epoch(model, Xtr, ytr)
    model.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        test_loss = F.cross_entropy(model(Xte), yte).item()
    if test_loss < best:
        best, best_state = test_loss, copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())
The test set chooses the epoch, so it has taken part in the training, and its result flatters the network. The validation set chooses the epoch; the test set is used once at the end.

6.

def augment(x):
    x = torch.flip(x, dims=[3])          # mirror left and right
    return x * (1 + 0.1 * torch.randn(len(x), 1, 1, 1))
A mirror image puts the right ventricle on the wrong side of the heart, a view that no patient produces. Small rotations, shifts and changes of gain keep the anatomy.

7.

# 40 abnormal frames among 400 test frames
acc = (pred == yte).float().mean().item()
print("Ready for the clinic, accuracy:", round(acc, 3))   # 0.9
With 10 percent abnormal frames, calling every frame normal already gives 0.900. The recall and the F1 of the abnormal class, by type of finding, show whether the network finds disease.

    Check yourself
    Seven questions for self-assessment. They are not graded.
    1. A neuron with ReLU has w = (1.0, −0.5) and b = 0.5 and receives x = (2.0, 1.0). What is its output?
    z = 1.0 × 2.0 − 0.5 × 1.0 + 0.5 = 2.0, and ReLU keeps a positive sum: 2.0. The sigmoid would give 0.881.
    2. For a patient with the label 1, a neuron with the sigmoid gives p = 0.8. What is the gradient of the cross-entropy for a weight whose input is 2.0?
    ∂L/∂w = (p − y) x = (0.8 − 1) × 2.0 = −0.4. The update w − η(−0.4) raises the weight, which raises p for this patient.
    3. A 3 × 3 convolution with stride 1 and no padding runs over a map of 32 × 32, followed by max pooling of 2 × 2. What size comes out?
    The convolution gives (32 − 3)/1 + 1 = 30, and the pooling ⌊(30 − 2)/2⌋ + 1 = 15.
    4. How many parameters does a convolution with 8 input channels, 4 kernels of 3 × 3 and biases have?
    Each of the 4 kernels has 8 × 3 × 3 = 72 weights and one bias: 4 × 72 + 4 = 292.
    5. The training loss keeps falling, while the validation loss rises from epoch 10. Which change does not fight overfitting?
    More epochs let the network learn its training frames even more by heart. Augmentation, dropout and early stopping all work against overfitting.
    6. A network flags 20 frames, 15 of them rightly, and misses 5 abnormal frames. What are its precision, recall and F1?
    P = 15/20 = 0.75 and R = 15/(15 + 5) = 0.75. When the two are equal, the F1 equals them: 2 × 15/(30 + 5 + 5) = 0.75.
    7. Why must model.eval() be called before a network with dropout and batch normalisation is measured?
    In training mode, dropout drops random units and batch normalisation uses the statistics of the batch. torch.no_grad() turns the gradients off; model.eval() does not.

    Weekly project
    What to do. Train the network of the echo laboratory on the short-axis slices of the cardiomyopathy clinic, with three classes: normal, dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM). First, split the 900 slices of make_cmr_set(300) into training, validation and test sets of 60, 20 and 20 percent, and normalise them with the training slices. Second, train EchoCNN(n_classes=3) with augmentation for up to 30 epochs and keep the weights of the epoch with the lowest validation loss. Third, report the confusion matrix, the precision, the recall and the F1 of each class, and the macro F1. Fourth, show every misclassified test slice with its measurements, and write which errors would matter most in the clinic and why.
    Where to look. Lecture: sections "A convolutional network for the echo laboratory", "Overfitting: Dropout, batch normalisation and augmentation" and "Reading the result: Precision, recall and F1". Interactive lab: Parts C, D and F. Jupyter notebook: Sections 5, 6, 7 and 10.
    Prompt route. The project can also be built with four prompts, one for each step, from the prompt library. The test of each prompt must hold before the next prompt is used.
    Example. Suppose the network calls 68 test slices DCM, 60 of them rightly, and misses none of the 60 slices with DCM: $TP = 60$, $FP = 8$, $FN = 0$. The precision of DCM is $60/68 = 0.882$ and its recall $60/60 = 1.000$. Its F1 is $2TP/(2TP + FP + FN)$, here $120/128 = 0.938$. The macro F1 is the plain mean of the F1 of the three classes.

Add the AI usage log (tool, prompt, what was checked, what was changed) and the Jupyter notebook with its outputs.
