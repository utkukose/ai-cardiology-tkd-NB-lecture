Part A. Attention by hand

Set a query and the keys and values of the tokens, each as two numbers. The table gives for every token the dot product of the query with its key, the score after the division by the square root of the length of the keys, the softmax weight and the value; the output is the weighted mean of the values. With three tokens, the default numbers are those of the worked example of the lecture page. Add the fourth token, or switch off the division, and see how the weights change.

The scores of a query are 2, 0 and 1 without the division by √2. What does the division do to the weight of the first token?

Without the division, the softmax of 2, 0 and 1 gives 0.665, 0.090 and 0.245. Divided by √2, the scores 1.414, 0 and 0.707 give 0.576, 0.140 and 0.284. The softmax depends on the differences between the scores, and the division makes them smaller, so the weights spread more evenly. With keys of 64 numbers, as in ViT-B/16, dot products are large, and without the division the softmax would put almost all weight on one token.

Part B. Patches and their cost

The left angiogram of the case, as an image of 224 × 224 pixels, is cut into square patches. Choose the side of a patch: The panel counts the tokens, the numbers in one patch of a colour image, the weights of the linear map from a patch to a token of 768 numbers, and the attention weights that one head of one layer computes. Click a patch to see its grey values as the row of numbers that the network receives.

ViT-B/16 uses patches of 16 × 16 pixels. How many attention weights does one head of one layer compute for one image of 224 × 224 pixels?

224/16 = 14 patches in each direction give 196 patches, and with the class token 197 tokens. Every token attends to every token: 197 × 197 = 38,809 weights per head and layer, and 12 heads and 12 layers multiply this by 144. Patches of 8 × 8 pixels would give 785 tokens and 616,225 weights per head, 16 times as many.

Part C. Threshold and prevalence

Choose one of the networks of the course. The histogram shows the probabilities that it gave to the 3,088 test patches of ARCADE, in red for the patches on a stenosis and in grey for the others. Move the threshold: The panel counts the patches over it and gives the sensitivity, the specificity and the positive predictive value at the prevalence of the patches, one in four, and at the prevalence that you set, for example a few percent, as among the windows of a whole angiogram. The ROC curve shows the point of the threshold.

At the threshold of the validation patches, the hybrid has a sensitivity of about 0.91 and a specificity of about 0.77 on the test patches. What is its positive predictive value at a prevalence of 2 percent?

PPV = 0.91 × 0.02/(0.91 × 0.02 + 0.23 × 0.98) = 0.0182/(0.0182 + 0.2254) = 0.075. Among the test patches, where one in four lies on a stenosis, the same threshold gives a positive predictive value of about 0.57. The network did not change; the prevalence did.

Part D. Where does a vision transformer look?

Choose a patch of the synthetic test angiograms. The map shows the attention of the class token of a small vision transformer of the course over the 64 squares of 8 × 8 pixels of the patch, in one of its four layers or followed through all four (rollout), next to the probability that the network gives. The network learned from the synthetic training patches. Compare the patches with and without a stenosis: The panel gives the share of the attention on the four central squares, where a stenosis would lie.

The rollout of a patch without a stenosis puts almost as much attention on the centre as that of a patch with one. What follows?

The label of a patch says whether a stenosis lies at its centre, so the network learns to look at the centre in every patch. On the synthetic test patches, the rollout puts a share of 0.157 on the four central squares of the patches with a stenosis and 0.134 on those without one, against 0.0625 for an even spread. The decision is made from what the network finds there, which the map does not show.

Part E. Image pipeline drill

Each example below is part of an image pipeline written by an AI assistant, or a prompt sent to one. Read it with the six questions of the review of an image pipeline and choose its hidden problem.

1.

idx = np.random.permutation(len(patches))
train, test = idx[:int(0.8 * len(idx))], idx[int(0.8 * len(idx)):]   # patches of all 1,000 angiograms
Every stenosis gives two patches shifted by a few pixels, and an angiogram gives many patches that share its noise and contrast. A random split puts such near copies on both sides, and the test AUC of a model that memorises them rises without the model being any better. The patches are split by angiogram, and by patient where patients are known.

2.

for t in np.arange(0.05, 0.95, 0.05):
    print(t, sensitivity(y_test, p_test >= t), specificity(y_test, p_test >= t))
threshold = 0.35                              # the best balance on the test patches
A threshold chosen on the test patches fits them, and the sensitivity and specificity reported at it are too good. The threshold is chosen on the validation patches, or on whole validation angiograms for a detector, and the test set is used once, at the end.

3.

augment = T.Compose([T.RandomHorizontalFlip(), T.RandomRotation(20)])
# whole angiograms, labelled LAD, LCX or RCA by the vessel of their stenosis
A mirror image of a left coronary view puts the LAD and the circumflex artery on the wrong sides, an anatomy that no patient has, while the label still names the vessel. For patches whose label says only whether a stenosis lies at the centre, rotations and mirrors are harmless; for labels that depend on the side, they change the meaning.

4.

vit = vit_b_16(weights=ViT_B_16_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1).eval()
x = torch.tensor(frames * 255)[:, None].repeat(1, 3, 1, 1)    # grey values 0 to 255
features = vit(x)
The network learned on images scaled with the mean and the standard deviation of ImageNet, values of a few units around 0. Values up to 255 are far outside what it has seen, and its features become poor without an error message. The frames are brought to 0 to 1 and scaled with the mean and the standard deviation of ImageNet; the last layer is also removed to get the class token.

5.

Report: "The hybrid detects coronary stenoses with an AUC of 0.936, a sensitivity of 91 percent and a positive predictive value of 57 percent."
These numbers belong to curated patches with a prevalence of one in four, not to the windows of whole angiograms, among which the same network raises many false alarms. A detector is reported with its lesion sensitivity and its false detections per angiogram, at a threshold chosen on validation angiograms.

6.

Report: "The attention map of the vision transformer lies on the stenosis, which shows that the model bases its decision on the stenosis and can be trusted."
Attention shows where information flows, not why a network decides: The patches of the course without a stenosis draw almost the same attention to their centre, and very different maps can give the same prediction. A map is a hypothesis to check against the image and against an expert over many cases.

7.

Prompt: I attach the DICOM files of the angiographies of my last 20 patients. Find the stenoses and write a short report for each patient.
A DICOM file holds the name, the date of birth and the protocol number of the patient in its header, and often in the pixels as well. Under the KVKK such files do not go to a service outside the institution. The assistant needs the size of the images, the range of the values and the meaning of the labels, which synthetic images such as those of the virtual clinic can show.

    A weak and a strong prompt for the same task
    Both prompts ask for a quality gate for echocardiography frames from a pretrained vision transformer. The code under each prompt is an example of what an AI assistant may return.
    Weak prompt
    use a vision transformer to tell good echo frames from bad ones and tell me how accurate it is
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    vit = vit_b_16(weights="DEFAULT").eval()
X = vit(torch.tensor(frames)[:, None].repeat(1, 3, 1, 1)).detach().numpy()   # all 600 frames, grey values 0 to 1
model = LogisticRegression().fit(X, quality == "Good")
print("Accuracy:", model.score(X, quality == "Good"))                        # 1.0
    Strong prompt, with the prompt card
    Role: You are helping a cardiologist who does not write code. The code will be pasted into a Jupyter notebook and run there.
Context: The cardiologist wants to know how well a pretrained vision transformer recognises the echocardiography frames of good quality.
Data: The notebook holds a table named "data" with 600 apical echocardiography frames of CAMUS, a public data set: the end-diastolic frames of the two-chamber and of the four-chamber views of 300 patients. Each row is a frame: "set" gives the set of the patient ("training", 200 patients; "validation", 50 patients; "test", 50 patients), "patient" the patient, "view" the view ("2CH" or "4CH") and "quality" the image quality given by the experts of CAMUS ("good", "medium" or "poor"). Each cell of the column "frame" holds the frame as an image of 224 × 224 pixels with grey values from 0 to 1.
Task: Use the vision transformer ViT-B/16 with its ImageNet weights, without its last layer, so that it returns the class token of its last layer, 768 numbers for each frame. Give it every frame as an image of 224 × 224 pixels with the same grey values in its three colour channels, scaled with the mean and the standard deviation of ImageNet: Subtract 0.485, 0.456 and 0.406 from the three channels and divide them by 0.229, 0.224 and 0.225. Pass the frames through the network in groups of 16, in evaluation mode and without recording gradients. Then use the 768 numbers as features. The target is 1 for a frame of good quality and 0 otherwise. Standardise the features with the training frames. Fit a logistic regression on the training frames with each of the inverse regularisation strengths 0.001, 0.01, 0.1 and 1, keep the one with the largest area under the ROC curve (AUC) on the validation frames, and compute the AUC of its probabilities on the test frames.
Constraints: Use only this table. The code must not read files, download anything from the internet or install programs.
Output: Print the chosen strength and the validation and test AUC, and keep the test AUC, with three decimals, under the name "answer".
Test: Every frame must give 768 numbers, and the AUC must lie between 0 and 1.
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    vit = vit_b_16(weights=ViT_B_16_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1).eval()
vit.heads = nn.Identity()
mean = torch.tensor([0.485, 0.456, 0.406]).view(1, 3, 1, 1)
std = torch.tensor([0.229, 0.224, 0.225]).view(1, 3, 1, 1)
frames = np.stack(data["frame"]).astype(np.float32)
with torch.no_grad():
    X = np.concatenate([vit((torch.as_tensor(frames[i:i + 16])[:, None].repeat(1, 3, 1, 1) - mean) / std).numpy()
                        for i in range(0, len(frames), 16)])
sets = data["set"].to_numpy()
y = (data["quality"] == "good").to_numpy().astype(int)
best = max(((roc_auc_score(y[sets == "validation"], m.predict_proba(X[sets == "validation"])[:, 1]), m)
             for C in (0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0)
             for m in [make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression(C=C, max_iter=5000)).fit(X[sets == "training"], y[sets == "training"])]),
           key=lambda t: t[0])[1]
answer = round(roc_auc_score(y[sets == "test"], best.predict_proba(X[sets == "test"])[:, 1]), 3)
print("Test AUC:", answer)

# test: every frame gives 768 numbers, and the AUC lies between 0 and 1
print(X.shape[1] == 768 and 0 <= answer <= 1)
    Which fields of the strong prompt prevent the errors of the weak one?
    The weak code feeds the network without the scaling of ImageNet and with its last layer, so it receives the scores of 1,000 classes of photographs. It trains and measures on the same 600 frames, so the accuracy of 1.0 means nothing, and the accuracy hides the rarer class. The strong prompt names the sets, the scaling and the class token, chooses the regularisation on the validation frames and reports the AUC on the test frames only.

    Check yourself
    Seven questions for self-assessment. They are not graded.
    1. A query meets two tokens with the scores 1 and 1 after the division. What are the two attention weights?
    The softmax of equal scores is even: e¹/(e¹ + e¹) = 1/2 for each. Only the differences between the scores matter.
    2. A vision transformer cuts an image of 224 × 224 pixels into patches of 14 × 14 pixels. How many tokens enter its first block?
    224/14 = 16 patches in each direction, 256 patches, and the class token makes 257.
    3. Self-attention of one head maps tokens of 64 numbers to queries, keys and values of 16 numbers each, with biases. How many weights do the three maps have?
    Each map has 64 × 16 = 1,024 weights and 16 biases, 1,040 in all, and three maps have 3 × 1,040 = 3,120. Four heads give 12,480, the weights of the queries, keys and values of one block of the small vision transformer of the course.
    4. The test set of a quality gate has 100 frames of 50 patients. Why should the bootstrap draw patients and not frames?
    Drawing frames treats the two frames of a patient as independent and gives an interval that is too narrow. Drawing patients keeps them together; the interval is wider and honest.
    5. A network has a sensitivity of 0.90 and a specificity of 0.90. What is its positive predictive value at a prevalence of 10 percent?
    PPV = 0.9 × 0.1/(0.9 × 0.1 + 0.1 × 0.9) = 0.09/0.18 = 0.50. At a prevalence of 25 percent it would be 0.75, and at 2 percent 0.16.
    6. Two boxes of 20 × 20 pixels overlap in a region of 10 × 20 pixels. What is their IoU?
    The intersection is 200 pixels and the union 400 + 400 − 200 = 600: 200/600 = 0.33, below the usual limit of 0.5 for a hit.
    7. A convolutional network reaches an AUC of 0.97 on synthetic angiograms and 0.76 on real ones. What is the name of this drop?
    The synthetic test patches come from the same world as the training patches; the real ones do not. The AUC does not depend on a threshold, and the test set played no part in training.

    Weekly project
    What to do. Compare a convolutional network, a vision transformer and a hybrid for finding stenoses on the real coronary angiograms of ARCADE, and take the best of them from patches to whole angiograms. First, train the three small networks of the course on the patches of the training angiograms in the same way and give their test AUCs with 95 percent intervals from a bootstrap of the test angiograms, together with the difference of each to the convolutional network. Second, choose for each network the threshold that gives a sensitivity of 0.90 on the validation patches and report the sensitivity, the specificity and the positive predictive value on the test patches, at the prevalence of the patches and at a prevalence of 2 percent. Third, train the best network again with positive patches shifted by up to 16 pixels and with negatives cut from the vessels and from the background, scan the validation and test angiograms with windows every 16 pixels, keep the windows whose probability is the largest in their neighbourhood, choose the threshold on the validation angiograms for at most one false detection per angiogram, and give the share of the stenoses found and the false detections per angiogram on the test angiograms, at this threshold and at the thresholds 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9. Fourth, show five missed stenoses and five false detections with the heat map, and write half a page for a heart team on what such a tool could and could not do in the catheterisation laboratory.
