Week 10 · Interactive lab: Clinical decision support systems (CDSS)

Prof. Dr. Utku Kose

Lecture pageJupyter notebook (GitHub)Week page

This interactive lab has six parts and two closing tabs. Part A is the bedside screen of the telemetry ward: the probability of AF of every minute, the alarm episodes and the strip of each. Part B builds the HL7 FHIR report of a patient and checks it. Part C asks you to decide on six alarm minutes while it counts how often you open the strip, and Part D runs a user test on twelve alarm minutes with a clock and a decision log. Part E is a drill on the review of a prototype, and Part F is a prompt workshop. All data of the lab are synthetic data of the virtual clinic.

How to use this lab. The lecture page sends you to each part in turn. At the end of every part, the link «Back to the lecture page» takes you back to where you left. The tabs «Check yourself» and «Reflect and export» come at the end of the week.

Part A. The bedside screen of the telemetry ward

Choose a patient of the ward. The chart shows the probability of AF of every minute of 24 hours from version 1.0 (red), the threshold (dashed line) and the AF of the recording (grey). Move the threshold and read the AF burden and the alarm episodes. Choose an alarm episode in the list to see the strip of its minute with the highest probability, its RR intervals, its five numbers and the contributions of the four numbers of version 1.0.

VC-0347 raises many alarm episodes but has no AF. What do the strips of its episodes show?

The four numbers of version 1.0 measure how much the intervals vary, and premature beats make them vary a lot. The strip shows the cause: a P wave before every beat, and sinus beats between the premature ones. The physician rejects such alarms, and the user test counts them.

Part B. The report as HL7 FHIR resources

The report of VC-0128 after 24 hours, built from the result of version 1.0: a Device, an Observation, a DiagnosticReport and a Provenance. Choose the decision of the cardiologist and watch the report change. Then break the report on purpose and run the checks: An information system rejects a report whose references do not resolve or whose unit is not a code of UCUM.

The cardiologist rejects the finding. Which elements does the report carry afterwards?
A rejection is a decision and belongs in the record: The report becomes final, it names the cardiologist in the DiagnosticReport and in the Provenance, and it no longer claims AF. Entered-in-error is kept for a report that should never have existed, such as one written for the wrong patient.

Part C. The physician decides
Six alarm minutes of the ward wait for your decision. The probability of the model comes first; the strip and the RR intervals appear only when you open them. Decide on each minute as you would on a busy shift, with or without the strip. At the end, the page counts the decisions that you made without opening the strip and the false alarms among them that you confirmed.
Which design of the screen makes automation bias less likely?
Goddard and colleagues found that workload and pressure of time make automation bias more likely, and that the way the advice is shown changes how strong it is. A screen that puts the evidence next to the advice, asks for a reason and makes a rejection cheap keeps the physician in the decision.
← Back to the lecture page: Part C is done; continue reading below its box. Practice stop 3 of the lecture page then works on this topic in the Jupyter notebook.
Part D. The user test
Twelve alarm minutes of the four patients of the ward, in random order, each with its strip, its RR intervals and the contributions of version 1.0. A clock runs for each minute. Confirm or reject each minute with a reason. At the end, the table compares your decisions with the truth of the recordings and with the two versions of the model, and the decision log can be exported as a file of comma-separated values.
In a user test, the physicians reject most alarms of one patient with the reason "premature atrial beats". What follows for the next version?
The decisions and their reasons name the cause of the false alarms. With version 1.0 the physician of the ward would review 80 alarm episodes in 24 hours, 76 of them false; a number that sees the pattern of premature beats, the sample entropy, cuts most of them, and the test records judge the new version once.

Part E. Review of a prototype
Each example below is a piece of a decision support prototype written by an AI assistant, or a prompt sent to one. Read it with the seven questions for reviewing a prototype and choose its hidden problem.
1.
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(minutes[NAMES], minutes["af"], test_size=0.25, random_state=1)
print("test AUC", roc_auc_score(y_test, LogisticRegression().fit(X_train, y_train).predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]))   # 0.99
The minutes of all 25 records are shuffled together, so the test holds minutes of the people that the model learned from, a few seconds apart from their training minutes. The AUC looks excellent and says little about a new patient. The records are split by person first, and the minutes follow their record.
2.
p_test = af_probability(model, test)
threshold = max(t for t in grid if (p_test[test["af"] == 1] >= t).mean() >= 0.95)   # chosen on the test records
print("test sensitivity", (p_test[test["af"] == 1] >= threshold).mean())
A threshold tuned on the test records makes the test sensitivity look exactly as planned, and the test no longer judges anything. The threshold is chosen on the validation records, and the test records are opened once, at the end.
3.
alarms = int((p_ward >= threshold).sum())
st.metric("Alarms today", alarms)          # VC-0519: 1439
Counting minutes turns one long episode of AF into 1,439 alarms in a day and buries the news. The unit that a physician reviews is the alarm episode, a run of consecutive alarm minutes, and the screen shows episodes per patient and day.
4.
# app_af.py
model = fit_model(V1_0, "1.0.0", load_training_minutes())   # fitted again each time the page opens
st.write("AF probability:", af_probability(model, table)[-1])
A model fitted again whenever the page opens may differ from one opening to the next, and no output can be traced to the model that produced it. The prototype reads a model file, shows its version and its fingerprint, and writes both into every output and every decision.
5.
st.metric("AF probability", f"{p:.2f}")
if st.button("Confirm AF"):
    log.append({"patient": pid, "decision": "confirmed"})     # no strip, no rejection, no reason
A screen with a probability and a single button invites automation bias: The physician cannot see the signal, cannot reject the alarm as easily as confirm it, and leaves no reason. The strip goes next to every alarm, and both decisions need a reason in the log.
6.
report = {"resourceType": "DiagnosticReport", "status": "final",
          "conclusionCode": [{"coding": [{"system": "http://snomed.info/sct", "code": "49436004"}]}]}   # written when the alarm rings
A final report with the code of AF, written by the software alone, puts a diagnosis into the record that no physician made. The report of the prototype stays preliminary until the decision of a physician, who is then named as performer and verifier.
7.
Prompt: Here are the beat times of my patient on ward 3 from last night, with her name and the date in the file name. Check my code for the AF burden on them.
Beat times with a name and a date are data on health of a person and personal data of a special category under the KVKK; they do not go to an AI assistant or to any service outside the institution. A prompt describes the columns and their units in words, and the code runs inside the institution.

Part F. Prompt workshop
Here a prompt takes the place of the code. Choose an exercise of the Jupyter notebook, write the seven fields of the prompt card in clinical language and watch the quality meter and the privacy check. The prompt names only the table "data" that the exercise cell prepares and the result "answer". Then copy the prompt into an AI assistant, paste the code it returns into the exercise cell in place of the line answer = None, and let the last line of the cell check the result.
    A weak and a strong prompt for the same task
    Both prompts ask for the sensitivity of version 1.1 on the test records. The code under each prompt is an example of what an AI assistant may return.
    Weak prompt
    what is the sensitivity of the new model
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    alarm = data["probability"] >= 0.5                       # a threshold that nobody chose
print("sensitivity:", alarm.mean())                      # the share of all minutes with an alarm
    Strong prompt, with the prompt card
    Role: You are helping a cardiologist who does not write code. The code will be pasted into a Jupyter notebook and run there.
Context: Before the clinic chooses a version, the cardiologist wants the sensitivity of version 1.1 on the test records, which neither version has seen.
Data: The notebook holds a table named "data" with the minutes of the 5 test records of the MIT-BIH Atrial Fibrillation Database, Holter recordings of people with mostly paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF), cut into windows of one minute. Each row is a minute: "record" gives the recording and "start (s)" the start of the minute in seconds; "AF" is 1 when more than half of the minute lies in AF as marked by the experts, and "probability" is the probability of AF from version 1.1 of a model of the course.
Task: A minute raises an alarm when its probability is at least 0.28. Compute the sensitivity, the share of the AF minutes with an alarm, and the specificity, the share of the other minutes without an alarm.
Constraints: Use only this table. The code must not read files, download anything from the internet or install programs.
Output: Print the two values, and keep the sensitivity, with three decimals, under the name "answer".
Test: Both values must lie between 0 and 1.
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    alarm = (data["probability"] >= 0.28).to_numpy()
af = data["AF"].to_numpy() == 1
sensitivity = alarm[af].mean()
specificity = 1 - alarm[~af].mean()
answer = round(float(sensitivity), 3)
print("sensitivity", answer, "| specificity", round(float(specificity), 3))

# test: both values lie between 0 and 1
print(0 <= sensitivity <= 1 and 0 <= specificity <= 1)
    Which fields of the strong prompt prevent the errors of the weak one?
    The weak code takes a threshold of 0.5 that nobody chose and divides the alarms by all minutes, so it reports the share of minutes with an alarm, not the sensitivity. The strong prompt gives the threshold of the validation records, 0.28, and defines the sensitivity as the share of the AF minutes with an alarm.

    Check yourself
    Seven questions for self-assessment. They are not graded.
    1. A minute has the RR intervals 0.6, 0.9 and 0.6 seconds. What is its pNN50?
    The two successive differences are 0.3 and −0.3 seconds, and both are larger than 0.05 seconds in absolute value: pNN50 = 2/2 = 1.
    2. Of ten consecutive minutes, minutes 1 to 2, 4 and 7 to 9 raise an alarm. How many alarm episodes are there, and what is the AF burden of the model?
    The runs are minutes 1 to 2, minute 4 and minutes 7 to 9: three episodes. Six of the ten minutes raise an alarm, a burden of 60 percent.
    3. A logistic regression has the intercept −2, and the contributions of a minute are 1.5, 0.8 and −0.3. What is its probability of AF?
    The log-odds are −2 + 1.5 + 0.8 − 0.3 = 0, and 1/(1 + e⁰) = 0.5.
    4. In a short minute, 7 pairs of single intervals are alike, and all 7 are still alike one interval later. What is its sample entropy?
    SampEn = −ln(A/B) = −ln(7/7) = 0: The pattern of the intervals repeats exactly, as in steady sinus rhythm.
    5. Which FHIR resource of the report names who wrote it and who verified it?
    The Provenance names the Device as the author and, after the decision, the cardiologist as the verifier; the DiagnosticReport also names the cardiologist as its performer.
    6. Under the guidance of the FDA of January 2026, why is software that finds AF in the RR intervals of a Holter recording a device?
    The first of the four criteria excludes software that analyses a medical image or a signal or pattern from a signal acquisition system, and the guidance gives the analysis of RR intervals from Holter recordings to find AF as an example of a device function.
    7. A new version cuts the false alarms and lowers the sensitivity. What belongs in its change log?
    A change log is written for the people who choose between the versions. It states the change, its reason, the threshold, the gain and the cost on the validation and the test records, and what still fails.

    Weekly project
    Project. User test session: Improving the CDSS prototype with feedback.
    What to do. Test the prototype of the course as its users would, and improve it. First, write down three tasks for a cardiologist on the telemetry ward, for example: find the AF burden of a patient, review the first five alarm episodes with their strips, and confirm or reject them in the decision log. Second, do the tasks with version 1.0 on record 08219 of the validation records and on the four patients of the ward, in the web interface or in the interactive lab, and record for every alarm episode the decision, its reason and the seconds it took. Third, count what the physician must review in 24 hours, the share of false alarm episodes and their causes. Fourth, improve the prototype: version 1.1 of the course with the sample entropy, or an improvement of your own, with its threshold chosen on the validation records only. Fifth, open the test records once for each version and give the AUC, the sensitivity, the specificity, the false alarm episodes and the error of the AF burden. Write a change log and the model card of the new version, and half a page on which version you would use on the ward, and what the physician must still check.
