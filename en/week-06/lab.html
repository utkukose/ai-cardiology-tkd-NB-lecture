This interactive lab has six parts and two closing tabs. Part A decides the assertion of the mentions of diagnoses in sentences of both hospitals, with the scope of the course and with a window, and Part B shows what the lower case does to the WordPiece tokens of a sentence. Part C computes TF-IDF weights by hand and opens the heaviest terms of the model of the course, and Part D reads an angiography report step by step, with switches for the steps of the pipeline. Part E is a drill on the review of a text pipeline, and Part F is the prompt workshop.

Part A. Negation, family history and uncertainty

Choose a sentence of the archive, or type one of your own. Every mention of the six diagnoses is marked, and for every mention you choose its assertion yourself. The panel then shows the decision of the rule of the course, which reads after the mention up to the end of the sentence or the next mention of another diagnosis, and the decision of a window of a fixed number of characters, whose size you set. The counter gives, for that window, the mentions of the test sets of both hospitals on which the window and the scope disagree.

Hospital B writes "KY (-) HT (+) DM (-)". What does a window of 40 characters decide for HT, and what does the scope of the course decide?

The window reads " (+) DM (-)." and the first words of the next sentence, so the "(-)" of diabetes negates hypertension. The scope stops at the next mention of another diagnosis, reads only " (+) " and keeps hypertension present. On the summaries of hospital B, a window of 40 characters and the scope disagree on 533 of 2,431 mentions.

Part B. Lower case and WordPiece tokens

Type a sentence of a report, in small or capital letters, or choose one of the examples. The panel shows the sentence as it is written, after Python's lower() and after the Turkish lower case, each with its WordPiece tokens and their numbers in the vocabulary of the tiny Turkish model, and it counts the tokens and the [UNK] tokens of each form. The interactive lab carries the 2,729 pieces of the 32,000 that the archive needs, and every single letter, so a word that the archive never writes may be cut into more pieces here than by the full tokenizer.

The tokenizer of the course does not change the case of the letters, and its vocabulary holds small letters only. What does it make of "PROKSİMAL" as written?

As written, the word is one [UNK]. After Python's lower() it becomes "pro ##ks ##i̇m ##al", with the hidden dot after the i, and after the Turkish lower case "pro ##ks ##ima ##l". Hospital B writes in capitals: As written, 19.5 percent of all its tokens are [UNK], and after the Turkish lower case none is.

Part C. TF-IDF by hand

The three short summaries are those of the worked example. Change them, or add a fourth summary of your own: The table gives for every term its document frequency, its idf and its weight in each summary after the scaling to length 1, and the panel gives the cosine of every pair of summaries. The second half shows the heaviest terms of the TF-IDF model of the course for one diagnosis. Mark the terms that a cardiologist would accept as evidence of the diagnosis.

In the first summary of the worked example, "izlenmedi" gets a larger weight than "atriyal". Why?

idf(izlenmedi) = ln(4/2) + 1 = 1.693 and idf(atriyal) = ln(4/3) + 1 = 1.288. Both occur once in the summary, so the weights differ by the idf alone; after the scaling to length 1 they are 0.681 and 0.518. TF-IDF does not know that "izlenmedi" negates the disease: It only finds the word rare.

Part D. Reading an angiography report step by step

Choose a report of hospital A or hospital B. The upper box shows the report with its annotated arteries, segments and stenoses. The lower box shows every sentence as the pipeline sees it: skipped, with the artery that it names, with the artery of the sentence before, or left out, together with the degrees of stenosis that it reads. Switch the four steps of the pipeline on and off: The table of the territories and the number of diseased vessels change next to the truth of the registry, and the counter gives the agreement over all test reports of both hospitals with the same switches.

A report says: "Sağ koroner arter (RCA) dominant ve iri. Proksimal segmentinde %90 darlık." What does the pipeline record for the RCA when the last switch is off?

Without the last switch, a sentence without an artery is left out, so the stenosis of 90 percent is lost and the patient loses a diseased vessel. With the switch, the sentence belongs to the RCA of the sentence before. Over the test reports of hospital A, the share of reports with the right number of diseased vessels falls from 0.959 to 0.901 without it.

Part E. Text pipeline drill

Each example below is part of a pipeline for clinical texts written by an AI assistant, or a prompt sent to one. Read it with the six questions of the review of a text pipeline and choose its hidden problem.

1.

low = summary.lower()
heart_failure = "kalp yetmezliği" in low      # hospital B writes "KALP YETMEZLİĞİ"
Python's lower() turns İ into an i followed by a hidden combining dot, so "KALP YETMEZLİĞİ" becomes "kalp yetmezli̇ği̇", and the search fails on every summary of hospital B. The Turkish lower case first replaces I by ı and İ by i and only then calls lower().

2.

clean = re.sub(r"\s+", " ", tr_lower(summary)).strip()
spans = [(m.start(), m.end()) for m in pattern.finditer(clean)]
mark(summary, spans)                            # the spans are drawn on the original summary
Joining runs of spaces and the strip() change the length of the text, so the positions found in clean no longer point to the same characters of the original summary: Every mark after the first double space is shifted. A pipeline that marks entities keeps the length of the text, as the Turkish lower case does, or keeps a map from the new positions to the old ones.

3.

vectorizer = TfidfVectorizer(min_df=2).fit(texts)      # all 3,600 summaries
X_train, X_ext = vectorizer.transform(texts[tr]), vectorizer.transform(texts[ex])
The vocabulary and the idf weights are learned from all summaries, those of the test set and of hospital B included. Words that only hospital B writes enter the vocabulary, and the external test is no longer external. The vectorizer is fitted on the training summaries only, and the thresholds are chosen on the validation summaries.

4.

before = low[max(0, start - 30):start]
negated = any(w in before for w in ["yok", "izlenmedi", "saptanmadı", "(-)"])
In Turkish the negation usually follows the disease, as in "atriyal fibrilasyon izlenmedi" or "HT (-)". A window before the mention misses it and catches the negation of the diagnosis before, as the "(-)" of KY in "KY (-) HT (+)". The negation is looked for after the mention, up to the end of the sentence or the next mention of another diagnosis.

5.

values = [int(v) for v in re.findall(r"%\s?(\d{1,3})", sentence)]
largest = max(values)                 # "%70-80 darlık" gives 70
The pattern reads only the first number of a range, so "%70-80" becomes 70 and "%60-70" becomes 60, which loses its significance; it also misses "yüzde 80" and the words "kritik" and "total oklüzyon". The rule of the course reads a range as its upper end, adds the words of stenosis, and keeps numbers of other kinds, a dose or the size of a catheter, apart.

6.

pred = model.predict(X_test)                          # six diagnoses at once
print("Accuracy:", (pred == Y_test).mean())            # 0.96
Most patients carry few of the six diagnoses, so a model that says "absent" almost everywhere reaches a high accuracy over all cells of the table. The result is given per diagnosis, with sensitivity, positive predictive value and F1, and on the summaries of hospital B as well as on those of hospital A.

7.

Prompt: Here are the discharge summaries of the 30 patients of my ward this week, with their names and protocol numbers. Read them and list the diagnoses of each patient.
A discharge summary is personal health data, with names, dates and numbers inside the text, and removing the name alone does not make it anonymous. Under the KVKK it does not go to a service outside the institution. The assistant needs the structure of the reports and synthetic examples, such as those of the virtual clinic, never the summaries of real patients.

    A weak and a strong prompt for the same task
    Both prompts ask for a search for diabetes in discharge summaries. The code under each prompt is an example of what an AI assistant may return.
    Weak prompt
    search the discharge summaries for diabetes and tell me how accurate it is
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    found = ["diyabet" in t.lower() or "dm" in t.lower() for t in texts]   # all 3,600 summaries
truth = archive["DM"] == 1
print("Accuracy:", np.mean(np.array(found) == truth))               # 0.676
    Strong prompt, with the prompt card
    Role: You are helping a cardiologist who does not write code. The code will be pasted into a Jupyter notebook and run there.
Context: The cardiologist wants to know how many of the alarms for diabetes of a search for words in discharge summaries are right.
Data: The notebook holds a table named "data" with the 600 discharge summaries of the test set of hospital A of the virtual clinic, a synthetic registry. Each row is a patient: "summary" holds the discharge summary in Turkish, and the six columns "HF", "AF", "CAD", "HT", "DM" and "CKD" hold 0 or 1, with 1 when the registry records heart failure, atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, hypertension, diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease, and 0 otherwise.
Task: Bring every text to small letters by the Turkish rules: First turn I into ı and İ into i, then make the other letters small. Call diabetes mellitus present when "diyabet", "diyabetes mellitus" or "dm" occurs anywhere in the summary as a whole word. Compare with the column "DM" and compute the positive predictive value.
Constraints: Use only this table. The code must not read files, download anything from the internet or install programs.
Output: Print the number of alarms and the positive predictive value, and keep the value, with three decimals, under the name "answer".
Test: The value must lie between 0 and 1.
    Example of code an assistant may return:
    def turkish_lower(text):
    return text.replace("I", "ı").replace("İ", "i").lower()

pattern = re.compile(r"(?<!\w)(diyabetes mellitus|diyabet|dm)(?!\w)")
alarm = np.array([bool(pattern.search(turkish_lower(t))) for t in data["summary"]])
truth = data["DM"].to_numpy() == 1
answer = round(float((alarm & truth).sum() / alarm.sum()), 3)
print("Alarms:", int(alarm.sum()), "positive predictive value:", answer)

# test: the value lies between 0 and 1
print(0 <= answer <= 1)
    Which fields of the strong prompt prevent the errors of the weak one?
    The field Data gives the 600 summaries of the test set and the column "DM" of the registry, so the assistant works on one set. The field Task asks for the Turkish lower case and for whole words, so "prediyabet" and the capitals of hospital B do no harm, and for the positive predictive value instead of the accuracy, which the many patients without diabetes inflate. The result, 0.497, then shows the limit of the method itself: Many alarms come from negated or family mentions, which the rule of Section 3 sets aside.

    Check yourself
    Seven questions for self-assessment. They are not graded.
    1. A hospital writes "SIRT AĞRISI" (back pain). What do Python's lower() and the Turkish lower case of the course give?
    Python's lower() follows the rules of English and turns the dotless capital I into a dotted i. The Turkish lower case of the course turns I into ı first, so only it gives the word of the list.
    2. In a collection of 4 summaries, a term occurs in 1. What is its idf with the formula of the course, ln((1 + n)/(1 + df)) + 1?
    ln((1 + 4)/(1 + 1)) + 1 = ln 2.5 + 1 = 0.916 + 1 = 1.916. A term that occurs in every summary gets ln(5/5) + 1 = 1, the smallest value.
    3. A rule gives 80 alarms for a diagnosis, and 60 of them are right. Of the patients, 75 carry the diagnosis. What is the F1 of the rule?
    Sensitivity 60/75 = 0.80, positive predictive value 60/80 = 0.75, F1 = 2 × 0.80 × 0.75/(0.80 + 0.75) = 1.20/1.55 = 0.774. F1 lies between the two and closer to the smaller one.
    4. In "kronik böbrek hastalığı nedeniyle", the first three words are one diagnosis. With one token per word, which tags does the BIO scheme give?
    B marks the first token of an entity and I the tokens inside it, so a diagnosis of three words is B I I. "nedeniyle" belongs to no entity and gets O. Three B tags would mean three separate diagnoses.
    5. The annotation of a summary has 10 entities. The model finds 9: 7 match exactly, and one of the others has the right type but loses its last suffix. What is the F1 by exact match?
    Exact match counts the shortened entity as a false alarm and its annotation as missed: precision 7/9 = 0.778, recall 7/10 = 0.700 and F1 = 2 × 7/(9 + 10) = 14/19 = 0.737.
    6. Two word vectors of length 1 are (0.6, 0.8) and (0.8, 0.6). What is their cosine?
    For vectors of length 1 the cosine is the dot product: 0.6 × 0.8 + 0.8 × 0.6 = 0.48 + 0.48 = 0.96. The two directions are nearly the same.
    7. A report says "LAD proksimalde %60-70 darlık, mid segmentte ileri derecede darlık". With the rules of the course, what does the LAD territory get?
    The range counts as its upper end, 70, and "ileri derecede" as 80. The territory keeps the largest value, 80, which falls into the category 70 to 89 and counts as significant.
